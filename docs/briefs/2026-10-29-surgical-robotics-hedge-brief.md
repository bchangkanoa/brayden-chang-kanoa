## Executive Summary

Our company is a U.S.-based surgical robotics manufacturer expecting a €9,400,000 payment from a German hospital group in 365 days. Since the company operates in U.S. dollars but will receive euros, it faces foreign exchange risk. If the euro weakens against the dollar before payment is received, the value of our receivable will decrease when converted into dollars. For example, a $0.10 decrease in the EUR/USD exchange rate would reduce the company's proceeds by $940,000. This potential reduction could significantly affect cash flow and profitability. We will evaluate forward contracts, money-market hedges, and euro put options to determine how the company can protect its future payment while considering potential upside.

## Background & Objectives

The primary objective is to protect the dollar value of the €9,400,000 receivable due in one year. A secondary objective is to assess whether retaining potential gains from a stronger euro is worth the additional cost or uncertainty. Our analysis will compare the guaranteed proceeds, costs, and possible outcomes of all three hedging strategies before making a final recommendation in Stage 5.

## Methods

A forward contract would allow the company to lock in an exchange rate today for the payment due in 365 days. This provides certainty about future dollar proceeds but prevents the company from benefiting if the euro strengthens. A money-market hedge would use borrowing and investing in different currencies to establish a predictable dollar value, although it requires financing and depends on available interest rates. Purchasing euro put options would protect against a declining euro while allowing the company to benefit if the euro appreciates, but requires an upfront premium.

**Hypothesis:** I expect the forward contract to provide higher guaranteed dollar proceeds than the money-market hedge because it locks in the exchange rate for the entire receivable without an upfront option premium. Using the scenario's indicative forward rate of $1.1807 per euro, a forward contract would secure approximately $11,098,580 if that quote is available.

**How I Would Know I Was Wrong:** If the money-market hedge locks in more dollars than the forward contract using comparable market inputs, the hypothesis would be disproven. We will separately test whether the put option's potential upside justifies its premium across future exchange-rate scenarios.

## Limitations & Next Steps

We assume the full €9,400,000 will be received in 365 days and that all three strategies are available. The $1.1807 forward rate is an indicative scenario input, not a verified live quote. Current exchange rates, interest rates, and option premiums must still be confirmed.
