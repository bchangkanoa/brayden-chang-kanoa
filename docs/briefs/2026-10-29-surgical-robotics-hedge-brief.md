Business Exposure
Our company is a U.S.-based surgical robotics manufacturer expecting to receive a payment of €9,400,000 from a German hospital group in 365 days. Since the company operates in U.S. dollars but will receive payment in euros, it faces foreign exchange risk. If the euro weakens against the dollar before payment is received, the value of our receivable will decrease when converted into dollars. For example, a $0.10 decrease in the EUR/USD exchange rate would reduce the company's proceeds by $940,000. This potential reduction could significantly affect the company's cash flow and profitability, making it important to evaluate strategies to protect the value of our future payment.
Hedging Alternatives
To reduce the company's foreign exchange risk, we will evaluate three possible hedging strategies: forward contracts, money-market hedges, and currency put options. A forward contract would allow the company to lock in an exchange rate today for the €9,400,000 payment due in 365 days. This provides certainty about future dollar proceeds but prevents the company from benefiting if the euro strengthens. A money-market hedge would utilize borrowing and investing in different currencies to establish a predictable dollar value for the future payment. However, this strategy requires financing and depends on available interest rates. Finally, purchasing euro put options would protect the company against a declining euro while allowing it to benefit if the euro appreciates. The main disadvantage is the upfront premium required to purchase the options.
What I Am Assuming
For the analysis, we will assume that the company receives the full €9,400,000 payment in 365 days and that all three hedging strategies are available. We will use the scenario's indicative forward rate of $1.1807 per euro as a preliminary input while verifying current exchange rates, interest rates, and option premiums in later stages.
